In-Class Practical Exercise: Computational Thinking

Learning Objectives

By the end of the activity, students should be able to:

Break a complex problem into smaller parts (decomposition).

Identify important information and ignore unnecessary details (abstraction).

Identify repeated features or behaviours (pattern recognition).

Develop a clear sequence of instructions to solve a problem (algorithmic thinking).

Explain how computational thinking can be applied to everyday problems.

Scenario: Making the Perfect Cup of Tea 

Your school wants to create a simple computer-controlled machine that can make a cup of tea.

The machine needs to know exactly what to do, but it can only follow clear instructions.

Part 1 – Decomposition

Working in pairs, break the task “Make a cup of tea” into smaller tasks.

Write down at least 8 separate steps.

For example:

Get a cup.

...

Challenge: Can you identify any steps that could themselves be broken into smaller tasks?

Part 2 – Abstraction

Imagine that a computer is controlling the tea-making machine.

The following information is available:

The colour of the cup

The amount of water required

The type of tea

The student's name

The temperature of the water

The colour of the student's shoes

Whether milk is required

The student's favourite football team

The brewing time

Task

Decide which information the machine actually needs.

Create two columns:

[TABLE]

Relevant information | Irrelevant information

 | 

 | 

Question: Why is it useful to ignore irrelevant information when solving a problem?

Part 3 – Pattern Recognition

Look at the following sequences:

Sequence A:2, 4, 6, 8, 10, __, __

Sequence B:5, 10, 15, 20, __, __

Sequence C:Monday, Wednesday, Friday, __, __

Task

Identify the pattern in each sequence and predict the next two items.

Then discuss:

How does recognising patterns help when designing computer programs?
