In-Class Exercise: European Legal, Ethical and Professional Issues in ICT

Overview

Students act as a review board for a fictional EU health-tech startup, applying EU law and professional codes to one case 

Learning outcomes. Identify which EU instruments apply to an ICT product, separate legal from ethical and professional duties, and justify a clear recommendation.

Phase | Time | Activity

Brief | 5 min | Tutor reads the scenario; groups get their task

Group work | 20 min | Each group answers its two questions

Board meeting | 15 min | Each group presents for 3 minutes

Debrief | 10 min | Whole-class discussion

Scenario: MediMind

MediMind, a 40-person Dublin startup, sells MindCheck, an AI app that triages mental-health symptoms for 300,000 users in Ireland, Germany, France and Poland. This month:

Data deal. A US insurer offers €2m for "anonymised" data that still includes age, postcode, symptoms and timestamps.

Model update. A retrained model under-refers over-65s to emergency care 12% more often. Marketing wants it live in two weeks.

Breach. Chat logs of 8,000 users were exposed for 11 days. Found Monday; it is now Wednesday and nobody has been told.

Forum. The peer-support forum has self-harm posts and users who appear to be under 16.

Whistleblower. Priya, a junior developer and BCS member, flagged the bias and was told to "stop being difficult".

The CEO asks the board: launch, delay, or change course?

Group tasks

Each group gives the board the law that applies, the ethical issue beyond it, and one recommendation.

[TABLE]

Group | Role | Event | Questions

1 | Data Protection Officer | Data deal | Is this data really anonymous under GDPR? Could it lawfully go to the US, and should it?

2 | AI Governance Lead | Model update | What AI Act risk tier applies, and what must happen before launch? Does the deadline matter?

3 | Security Officer | Breach | When must the regulator and users be told? What should the user notice say?

4 | Trust and Safety Manager | Forum | What does the DSA require once harmful posts are known? How should under-16s be handled?

5 | Professional Standards Adviser | Whistleblower | Which BCS or ACM code clauses apply? How should Priya escalate, and what protects her?

Debrief

Was anything legal but still wrong, or unethical but legal?

Does EU regulation protect users or push innovation out of Europe?

If you were Priya, what would you do on Monday?

Tutor notes (remove before handing out)

The best answer is "change course": refuse the sale, delay the update, report the breach now, and protect Priya. Check current texts on EUR-Lex, as AI Act timelines have faced amendment proposals.

[TABLE]

Group | Key points

1 | Age + postcode + timestamps allow re-identification, so it is personal data (Recital 26). Health data is special category (Art 9). US transfer needs a certified Data Privacy Framework recipient or SCCs.

2 | Triage software is a medical device (MDR), so its AI is high-risk (AI Act Art 6(1)). Needs risk management, bias testing, human oversight and conformity assessment. A known harm outweighs a trade show.

3 | Regulator (Irish DPC) within 72 hours of awareness: deadline Thursday (Art 33). Health logs mean high risk, so users must be told (Art 34).

4 | Must act quickly on known illegal content and offer notice-and-action (DSA Arts 6, 16). Parental consent under 16 by default; France uses 15 (GDPR Art 8).

5 | BCS section 1 (public interest) outranks duty to employer; ACM 1.2 (avoid harm). Whistleblower Directive favours internal, then regulator; the press only in limited cases.
